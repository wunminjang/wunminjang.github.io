About

I study what people actually learn when AI helps them do the work.

I am an interdisciplinary researcher working across the learning sciences, educational psychology, human–AI interaction and computer-supported collaborative learning. My doctoral research at the Technical University of Munich uses controlled experiments, eye tracking and interaction analytics to separate how well people perform with assistance from what they can do independently afterwards.

Before Munich, I worked on applied education and workforce-development research in Korea, covering vocational instructor training, higher-education learning outcomes and digital-skills curricula. My PhD in Social Sciences and Technology was submitted in September 2026, with the defence expected in early 2027.

Research

Current and recent projects

2023–2026

AI assistance, task performance and learning

A controlled comparison of GitHub Copilot Chat and a curated Python tutorial. Participants solved 44 assisted programming tasks, with unassisted pre- and post-tests that separate performance during tool use from later independent performance. I recorded code submissions, interface interactions, eye movements, workload (NASA-TLX) and user experience (UEQ).

Copilot users reached markedly higher correctness on the assisted tasks, but there was no statistically detectable difference in short-term unassisted learning gains between conditions.

  • 61 analysed participants
  • 44 assisted tasks
  • GEE · mixed-effects models
  • Under review, IEEE TLT
2023–2026

Visual attention and communication in distributed pair programming

Exploratory studies of remote programming pairs (expert–expert, student–student and mixed-expertise) working on easy and difficult debugging tasks. Screen-based eye tracking, verbal interaction data, focus groups and questionnaires show how attention, shared focus and coordination shift with expertise and task difficulty.

  • 19 participants
  • Tobii Pro Lab · AOI analysis
  • ACM ETRA 2024
  • IEEE ITET 2026, Best Presentation
2024

Human versus LLM annotation of collaborative discourse

Compared human coding with zero-shot LLM multi-label annotation of transactive communication in pair-programming transcripts, using a nine-category scheme. The best LLM configuration reached about 0.59 F1, which points to clear limits for automated coding of educational dialogue.

  • 9 discourse categories
  • F1 ≈ 0.59
  • IEEE ITHET 2024
2026

Machine-learning eye tracking in education: a systematic review

A review of machine-learning approaches to gaze analysis in educational research, focused on whether analytic outputs connect to concrete pedagogical decisions. Written with international co-authors.

  • Under review, ACM TOCE
2021–2023

Applied education and vocational research, Korea

Team projects at Ewha Womans University and commissioned national studies: a quality-management framework for vocational instructors' in-service training, learning outcomes by university admission pathway, and competency standards and curricula for emerging digital occupations such as web programming and 3D modelling.

  • Focus groups · SEM · instrument validation
  • 10 co-authored journal articles

    Methods

    How I work

    Study design

    Controlled human-participant experiments, between-group comparisons, pre/post learning measures, rubric-based scoring, ethics and consent protocols, recruitment and scheduling.

    Multimodal data

    Tobii eye tracking and Tobii Pro Lab, area-of-interest and gaze-allocation analysis, interaction logs, verbal transcripts, NASA-TLX and UEQ.

    Statistics

    GEE, mixed-effects models and GLMM, regression, ANOVA, chi-square tests, structural equation modelling, equivalence bounds and instrument validation.

    Tools & qualitative work

    Python, SPSS, AMOS; focus-group interviews, discourse coding and mixed-method triangulation.
